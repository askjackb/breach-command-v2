# Acceptance and review — appearance is a deliverable

## First production batch

Create the base Autocannon, the Skitter, the reactor, a basalt surface and one fractured outcrop first. This batch establishes mechanical grammar, biological grammar, material response, environmental scale and movement together. Present them in one playable representative scene before producing all variants. The current game's placeholder visuals are not approval of a direction.

The user or designated art reviewer approves the look. A producing agent may label a submission `candidate`; it must not self-certify that the user has accepted the StarCraft-quality benchmark. Passing a format validator or triangle budget does not mean the art is good.

## Mandatory evidence for each candidate

- Neutral, ungraded turnaround with all sides visible; wireframe and material-channel views.
- Top/side/front view with dimensions and pivot/socket markers.
- In-engine gameplay view at normal zoom, nearest allowed zoom and distant overview. Include one portrait and one landscape view, not only a cropped beauty shot.
- Animated loop for moving units and mechanisms. Show foot contact, aiming and recoil as applicable.
- Comparison against the parent variant and at least two sibling classes; use equal scale, light and camera.
- Delivery report listing defects, missing pieces, source provenance and measured resource use. Export/import the actual asset; do not replace it with a separate prerender for evidence.

## Review questions

### Form and manufacturing/anatomy

Does the large silhouette communicate its specific job? Are supported shapes, joints and attachments believable? Is geometric detail concentrated where it improves the form? Are top-visible components deliberate? Are rounded surfaces appropriately curved rather than visibly polygonal? Is anatomy differentiated beyond color/scale? Are bosses anatomically new designs?

### Materials

Can the viewer distinguish paint, metal, rubber, ceramic, glass, horn and tissue under neutral light? Are roughness transitions plausible? Are bevels and normals credible? Does weathering follow contact, heat and dust instead of uniform noise? Is the asset visible in the battlefield lighting without baking an artificial glow into it? Are texture seams, UV stretching, mip shimmer and alpha halos absent?

### Animation and effects

Does locomotion have weight and correct contact at the game's speed? Does the turret rotate on its actual bearing? Does recoil return without snapping? Does a projectile emerge from a real muzzle? Can the player see what hit whom? Are death remains brief/pooled enough for an endless run? Does an effect remain legible when many weapons fire together?

### Playability and consistency

Can tower families, enemy roles and final specializations be distinguished on a phone? Does free rotation reveal unfinished geometry? Do assets respect current collision/placement constraints? Are UI tap targets separate from decorative artwork? Are signals understandable without depending solely on color? Does procedural environment assembly avoid invisible barriers or implied fixed lanes?

## Reject explicitly

A flat concept image presented as a 3D model; a detailed front with an unfinished back; default primitives presented as a finished industrial design; generic texture noise used to hide a poor silhouette; a smooth pebble rock pack; bright plastic; cartoon/cute proportions; oversized neon strips; one common insect body reused as all roles; upgrades that only change tint; decorative mechanisms implying attacks or abilities the game does not have.

## Mobile performance evidence

Profile the actual iPhone 17 browser in portrait and landscape. Target sustained 30 FPS with representative towers, map items, 110 enemies and mixed weapon effects. Report frame-time distribution, memory, load time, effect counts, thermal behavior during several minutes of play and device/browser/build version. Headless desktop screenshots do not establish this target. Choose LOD distances and effect limits from measurement while preserving appearance at allowed zoom levels.

## Decision record

For each asset record: candidate version, reviewer, date, approved dimensions, appearance notes, import defects, performance notes, status and follow-up. Keep rejected versions distinct. An approved style anchor should guide the remaining roster; review distinctive units and bosses again rather than assuming consistency is automatic.
